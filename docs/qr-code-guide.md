# QR Code Guide

This guide shows how to create QR codes for your own projects or for clients.

## What a QR Code Is

A QR code is a scannable image that opens a link when someone scans it with a phone camera.

You can use it for:

- website homepage
- contact page
- WhatsApp link
- project inquiry page
- portfolio page
- app download page

## Basic Workflow

Use this same process every time:

1. choose the exact link
2. generate the plain QR code
3. place it on a simple branded design
4. export as PNG
5. test it with your phone

## Step 1: Choose the Link

Decide what the QR code should open.

Examples:

- homepage
- contact form
- WhatsApp chat link
- booking page
- payment page

Always copy the final correct link before generating the QR code.

## Step 2: Generate the Plain QR Code

Use a QR code generation website.

Common options:

- https://www.qr-code-generator.com/
- https://www.the-qrcode-generator.com/
- https://goqr.me/
- https://www.canva.com/

What to do:

1. paste the link
2. generate the QR code
3. download it as PNG

Good file names:

- `website-qr-code.png`
- `contact-qr-code.png`
- `whatsapp-qr-code.png`

## Are QR Generation Sites Free?

Some are free, some are partly free, and some limit features.

Safe rule:

- basic static QR codes are usually free
- advanced features may require payment

Basic free use often includes:

- website URL QR code
- PNG download
- simple black and white QR code

Features that may require payment:

- dynamic QR codes
- scan tracking and analytics
- custom branding
- editable destination after creation
- higher-resolution export on some platforms

## Static vs Dynamic QR Codes

### Static QR Code

This is the normal one.

- free on many sites
- opens one fixed link
- simple and reliable

Use this for most websites and client work.

### Dynamic QR Code

This is more advanced.

- can change destination later
- may include scan analytics
- often requires a paid plan

Use this only if the client specifically needs tracking or editable links.

## My Recommendation

For most future work:

- use `goQR` for fast plain QR code generation
- use `Canva` if you want to place the QR code into a branded layout

That is a strong and low-cost workflow for both your own projects and client jobs.

## Step 3: Create a Branded Card

After downloading the plain QR code, place it inside a simple design.

You can use:

- Canva
- PowerPoint
- Figma
- Photoshop

Recommended text:

- `SCAN TO VISIT`
- project or brand name
- `Scan with your phone camera`

Keep the design clean and minimal.

## Step 4: Export the Final Design

For sharing:

- export as `PNG`

For printing:

- export as `PNG` or `PDF`

PNG is best for:

- WhatsApp
- email
- social media
- quick client delivery

## Step 5: Test the QR Code

Before sending it to anyone:

1. open the image on another screen or device
2. scan it with your phone
3. confirm it opens the correct link
4. confirm the image is not blurry

Never send an untested QR code to a client.

## Best Design Tips

- keep enough white space around the QR
- do not stretch or distort it
- do not make it too small
- do not put too much text around it
- use high contrast, usually black on white
- keep branding simple

## Recommended Sizes

For digital sharing:

- 1000px or more is good

For print:

- use a high-resolution PNG
- test print if possible

## What to Ask a Client

If a client wants a QR code, ask:

1. what link should it open?
2. what brand name should be shown?
3. where will it be used?
4. do they want square or horizontal layout?
5. do they want just the QR or a branded card too?

## Good Layout Types

### Square Layout

Best for:

- WhatsApp
- Instagram
- social sharing
- flyers

### Horizontal Layout

Best for:

- business cards
- banners
- printed cards

## Simple Repeatable Checklist

1. copy the final link
2. generate the plain QR code
3. download as PNG
4. place it in a branded design
5. export the final image
6. test scan it
7. send it to the client

## Final Advice

For most future work, use a free static QR code first.

That is usually enough for:

- your own projects
- client websites
- business cards
- flyers
- WhatsApp contact sharing

Only move to paid dynamic QR tools if the client needs analytics or link editing after printing.
